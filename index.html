Overview

The poster examines how single and competing pure tones shape oscillation frequencies across a tonotopically arranged oscillator bank. Tonotopy is stimulus-dependent: the response at a given position depends on the input. Local parameter adaptation can improve target–masker separation, suggesting a possible role for efferent adaptation.

Model

The delay differential equation (DDE) describes a local cochlear partition with displacement \(x\), velocity \(v\) and delayed active feedback. With \(x_\tau=x(t-\tau)\), its cubic feedback approximation is

\[\dot{x}=v,\qquad \dot{v}=-\frac{k}{m}x-\frac{d}{m}v-\frac{1}{m}\left[\Psi(0)+\Psi'(0)x_\tau+\frac{\Psi''(0)}{2}x_\tau^2\right]x_\tau+s(t)\]

Near a Hopf bifurcation, the dominant oscillatory dynamics of this delayed system can be approximated by a Stuart–Landau oscillator (SLO). The complex state \(\eta\) combines displacement and velocity; the effects of the feedback strength and delay enter the coefficients \(\mu\), \(\omega_0\), \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) through the correspondences below. This gives a local approximation with amplitude–phase dynamics and no explicit delay, which can be coupled to other SLOs to form an oscillator bank.

\[\dot{\eta}=(\mu+j\omega_0)\eta-(\alpha+j\beta)|\eta|^2\eta+s(t)\]

Coupling between elements is added to each local oscillator equation.

DDE–oscillator correspondences

LINEAR DYNAMICS
\[\mu=\frac{\Psi(0)}{m\tilde{\omega}_0}\cos(\tilde{\omega}_0\tau)-\frac{d}{m}\]
ANGULAR FREQUENCY
\[\omega_0=\tilde{\omega}_0+\frac{\Psi(0)}{m\tilde{\omega}_0}\sin(\tilde{\omega}_0\tau)\]
CUBIC COEFFICIENTS
\[-\alpha-j\beta=\frac{3}{2m\tilde{\omega}_0}\Psi''(0)e^{-j\tilde{\omega}_0\tau}\]
Parameter notation
\(\Psi,\;\Psi''(0)\)
Active-feedback function and its second derivative at zero
\(m,\;d,\;\tau\)
Effective mass, damping coefficient and feedback delay
\(\tilde{\omega}_0,\;\omega_0\)
Reference angular frequency of the delayed system and linear angular frequency of the oscillator
\(\mu,\;\alpha,\;\beta\)
Linear dynamics, nonlinear amplitude and amplitude–phase coupling parameters
\(j\)
Imaginary unit

For the following plots, the oscillator bank is driven by two pure tones at 303 Hz and 360 Hz with equal amplitudes. The instantaneous frequency describes how fast an oscillator’s phase changes at each moment: \(f_{\mathrm{inst}}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{d\theta(t)}{dt}\), where \(\theta(t)\) is its unwrapped phase. The first plot shows this frequency over time for every oscillator; the second shows the corresponding amplitude \(|\eta(t)|\).
