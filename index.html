Overview

The poster examines how single and competing pure tones shape oscillation frequencies across a tonotopically arranged oscillator bank. Tonotopy is stimulus-dependent: the response at a given position depends on the input. Local parameter adaptation can improve target–masker separation, suggesting a possible role for efferent adaptation.

Model

The delay differential equation (DDE) describes a local cochlear partition with displacement x, velocity v and delayed active feedback. With xτ = x(t − τ), its cubic feedback approximation is

x˙=v,v˙=−kmx−dmv−1m[Ψ(0)+Ψ′(0)xτ+Ψ″(0)2xτ2]xτ+s(t)

Near a Hopf bifurcation, the dominant oscillatory dynamics of this delayed system can be approximated by a Stuart–Landau oscillator (SLO). The complex state η combines displacement and velocity; the effects of the feedback strength and delay enter the coefficients μ, ω₀, α and β through the correspondences below. This gives a local approximation with amplitude–phase dynamics and no explicit delay, which can be coupled to other SLOs to form an oscillator bank.

η˙=(μ+jω0)η−(α+jβ)|η|2η+s(t)

Coupling between elements is added to each local oscillator equation.
